# Pocket Dimension visual target

The accepted [imagegen mockup](../screenshots/ui-themes/pocket-dimension-polish-concept.png) is the visual target. It is a design reference, not a screenshot of the native build.

The implementation should preserve the fine molded-plastic shell, deep LCD bezel, readable six-slot pixel browser, balanced monospaced title, large photo frame, quiet aligned metadata, and clear artwork/Play controls. Artwork, browser selection, navigation, and launch controls remain native widgets; the full mockup must never be used as the application background.

Theme-system capabilities must remain optional and package-independent. Images, fonts, colors, geometry, and actions belong in the package; the renderer supplies generic behavior.

The subsequent polish pass uses the existing Hollycast logo at its original aspect ratio, in a larger toolbar slot. The selected game's artwork sits in a recessed sage display, with local artwork providing a subtle backdrop around portrait covers. A native scrollable details region exposes all available metadata and the description, omits missing values, and preserves access in the portrait layout. Toolbar image sizing, fallback artwork, and metadata panels are reusable opt-in capabilities.

Generated with the built-in imagegen tool using this prompt and the earlier Pocket Dimension concept as a reference:

> Use case: ui-mockup
> Asset type: a highly polished, shippable desktop game-library interface for the Hollycast Dreamcast emulator, theme name Pocket Dimension.
> Input image 1 is a CONCEPT REFERENCE, not a finished design to copy literally. Preserve the core idea: a Dreamcast VMU handheld on the left is the actual game browser, and a full-color selected-game preview occupies the right. Rework its materials, proportions, spacing, alignment, typography, controls, and visual hierarchy to look professionally art-directed and considerably more polished.
>
> Create ONE complete 16:10 landscape app screen, high-resolution, edge-to-edge UI with no surrounding device, browser frame, mockup presentation board, annotations, or promotional text. It must look like software somebody could use, not a photograph of a desk and not a landing page.
>
> Art direction: sophisticated late-1990s Japanese consumer electronics, precise industrial design, calm and tactile, playful but restrained. An airy pale warm-gray background, subtle warm ivory molded plastic, muted sage and olive, deep moss text. LCD glass has authentic muted green four-tone pixel graphics. Extremely subtle surface grain and soft, physically coherent contact shadows, well defined bevels and gentle highlights. Use space and proportion to create depth. Avoid chunky toy-like extrusion, excessive neumorphism, heavily yellow paper, hazy low contrast, elaborate gradients, fake grunge, scattered decorations, and generic dashboard cards.
>
> Top app navigation: a slim clean toolbar with a small Hollycast mark and exact word "Hollycast" on the left. On the right, restrained controls with exact labels "All games", "Continue playing", "Favourites", followed by simple search and settings icons. All games is selected in a tasteful sage pill. A thin subtle divider separates toolbar and content.
>
> Main layout: generous and precisely balanced, main content occupies almost all remaining space. The VMU uses roughly the left third, shown near-front-on with at most a tiny natural tilt. Its silhouette should feel convincingly like a classic Dreamcast VMU: a small top connector, beautiful ivory shell, dark sage screen bezel, LCD, one raised cross-shaped directional pad, two round A and B buttons, small bottom attachment slot. Refine the casing proportions and manufacture details: consistent thin bevels, smooth large corner radii, understated recessed seams, realistic but subtle button depth. No extra knobs or impossible hardware. Small tasteful text on the device reads "HOLLYCAST" and "POCKET DIMENSION".
>
> The LCD is ACTIVE AND USABLE: a compact "GAME SELECT" header and a three-column, two-row grid of six distinctly readable monochrome pixel-art game icons. Under the icons show the short labels "CT2", "TAXI", "DOA2", "CvS", "PSO", "RALLY". A thin dark rectangular selection clearly highlights CT2, while preserving the pixel art's contrast. This is a game picker inside the screen, not a photo of miniature cover thumbnails. Match tiny pixel spacing consistently. Keep all six cells within the screen. A and B hardware labels are readable.
>
> Right-hand panel: keep it visually integrated with the overall interface, without a filled card behind the whole region. An understated "Selected game" label, then the title "Crazy Taxi 2" in a beautifully spaced dark moss monospaced or technical rounded sans typeface. The heading is confident but not huge or stretched. Below it, a large sharp full-color Crazy Taxi 2 gameplay preview featuring a yellow taxi on a city street, using the reference screen as content guidance. It sits in a refined, thin, warm-white archival photo border with a slightly deeper bottom margin. One very subtle translucent tape tab may anchor the top; maximum one degree tilt. The photo should be comfortably large and aligned, not floating awkwardly or intruding on the device.
> Below, present a quiet aligned metadata row: "Hitmaker" / "Driving" / "2001", with measured spacing rather than long text. Add a small "Cover" / "Screenshot" segmented choice, with Screenshot selected, and a clear sage "Play" button with a small play triangle aligned to the lower right of this region. Under the device show understated control guidance: "Browse" with left/right arrows, "A Cover", "B Screenshot".
> Maintain very crisp typography, readable secondary text, optical alignment, consistent 8-pixel spacing rhythm, comfortable hit targets and disciplined margins. All meaningful text should render exactly, with no lorem ipsum, made-up metrics, duplicate controls, watermarks or invented labels.
>
> The result should preserve the charm of a physical VMU and a game photograph, while looking far more intentional, coherent, refined and ready to implement than the reference.

Device asset prompt:

> Use case: background-extraction
> Asset type: a transparent PNG hardware skin for an interactive desktop app.
> Reference image 1 is the accepted Pocket Dimension interface design. Extract and recreate ONLY the ivory VMU-shaped handheld on its left as a pristine, high-resolution, near-front-on isolated asset. Match its refined industrial design faithfully: the same gently rounded rectangular ivory shell, very fine molded seams, subtle warm-plastic texture, raised sage connector at the top, deep dark sage LCD bezel, recessed pale-green LCD screen, beautiful bevels and physically coherent gentle highlights, a dark sage cross-shaped d-pad, two pale-sage round A and B buttons, and the small attachment slot at the bottom. Keep the careful materials, shell proportions, restrained shadow, and labeling from the reference. The device should be straight-on and vertically aligned with no rotation or perspective skew, so native UI regions can be positioned precisely inside it.
> Remove ALL content from the LCD except a uniform blank muted LCD green background with extremely subtle pixel-matrix texture. In particular remove GAME SELECT, the 06 counter, all icons, all thumbnails, all selection rectangles, all rules and all small labels from the LCD. This entire green screen area is to be filled by live application UI later. The shell labeling "HOLLYCAST" and "POCKET DIMENSION" stays, and the A and B letters stay. The d-pad may retain its four very small triangle marks.
> The whole asset contains only the hardware device and a very subtle contact shadow. The rest MUST be truly transparent alpha, including the area surrounding the device. No desktop, no paper background, no toolbar, no game image, no metadata, no floating controls, no caption, no frame around the asset. Preserve the soft contact shadow in alpha without gray or white matte edges. Do not replace transparency with a checkerboard drawing.
> Composition: vertical image, roughly a 2:3 width-to-height canvas, with the entire device in view. The device silhouette should occupy most of the canvas with a small consistent transparent safety margin. High resolution and crisp detail suitable for a desktop UI at 2x scale. This is a production image asset, not a mockup board.
