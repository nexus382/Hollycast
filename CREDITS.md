# The people behind Hollycast

**Contributors, sources, and a few overdue thank-yous.**

There are a lot of people's hours in this project. This page is for them.

## Flyinghead & the Flycast contributors

Hollycast wouldn't be here without [flyinghead](https://github.com/flyinghead) and [Flycast](https://github.com/flyinghead/flycast). His many years of work are the foundation we build on every day.

Dreamcast, Atomiswave, Naomi, Naomi 2, System SP: getting this hardware to run well takes an enormous amount of work. Getting it accurate, compatible, and efficient, then continuing to improve it year after year, takes a level of dedication that deserves real recognition.

It's easy to load a game, have it work, and never think about how much had to happen to make that possible. Flyinghead has spent years doing that work. The compatibility, performance, and care people associate with Flycast didn't happen by accident.

To him, the Flycast contributors, and the people behind reicast and nullDC before it: thank you. Whatever we add to Hollycast, we know how much of it starts with you.

On a personal level, Flycast has brought me immense happiness. Being able to play the titles I grew up with and relive those childhood memories means a lot to me. It's a big part of why I wanted to put my time into Hollycast. If this project can give even one more person that same happiness, then I consider the endless hours I've invested worth it.

## Tovarichtch, ODIN & the Hikaru work

Hikaru is a difficult system to emulate. Having other people's research and experience to learn from has mattered a great deal.

**Réda Chérif-Touil (Tovarichtch)**, the developer of [ODIN](https://github.com/Tovarichtch/Odin), deserves a special thank-you. The debugging information his code provided, helped us work through our own Hikaru emulation, including understanding the Valkyrie code we used as a reference. That help made a real difference to our progress on a system that is very difficult to get right.

I appreciate the time you've put into ODIN, and the help your code has provided us. You had your own project to spend that time on, and you still helped ours move forward with that very same hardwork. That means a lot to me.

ODIN deserves recognition as a project in its own right, too. I'm glad it exists, and I'm glad the community has another developer working to understand Hikaru and give people a way to enjoy its games. I'd encourage anyone interested in the system to take a look at [ODIN and the work going into it](https://github.com/Tovarichtch/Odin).

Credit also belongs to **Stefano Teso**, the author of [Valkyrie](https://github.com/stefanoteso/valkyrie), the earlier Hikaru emulator from which ODIN was forked. His work gave later developers something concrete to study when trying to understand this hardware.

Having that work available, and people willing to help explain it, has saved us time and helped us make sense of a very challenging machine. Tovarichtch's support is a good example of what I value about this community: people making their own projects better while helping someone else make progress too.

## MAME & the people documenting the hardware

[MAME](https://github.com/mamedev/mame) has also been a major reference for our Hikaru work. Its source and hardware notes hold years of research that would be much harder to piece together alone.

Thank you to the MAME developers and contributors, including **MetalliC**, credited on the [Hikaru driver](https://github.com/mamedev/mame/blob/master/src/mame/sega/hikaru.cpp), and everyone who helped investigate and document the system. That knowledge has been a real help to us.

## To everyone playing Hollycast

You are a big part of what makes this community great. So many of you are already enjoying Hollycast, and just about everything we've heard back has been positive. People have been encouraging, patient, and willing to help. With the way things often go on the internet these days, that's been a welcome change of pace. It makes me want to keep working on this.

There have been a couple of negative interactions too, including some that felt like complaining just for the sake of it. Even those moments give us a chance to decide how we want to treat each other. We can disagree, stay positive, and still work together to help something good grow. We don't all have to want the same things from an emulator to be part of the same community.

No emulator will please every user, and Hollycast won't be everyone's first choice. That's fine. The whole point was to give another option to a community that has done so much and has always been there for us. If Hollycast gives you a way to enjoy these games that suits you, then it's doing what I hoped it would.

Thank you for giving it a chance, for telling us what you enjoy and what needs work, and for making the community around it a good place to be.

## A community worth being part of

I'm glad players have more than one emulator to choose from. Different projects have different strengths, and there is room for all of us to keep improving.

The emulation scene is as good as it is because people do their share of the work and help each other along. They share findings, answer questions, test someone else's build, and offer support when progress gets difficult. That helps developers make their own applications better and gives players better options.

To everyone who has contributed code, tested a build, reported a problem, helped another user, or offered some encouragement: thank you. There is a lot of work here that doesn't show up in a commit history. It still counts.

## A personal thank-you to Webcoder

Without Webcoder, this project couldn't be where it is today.
